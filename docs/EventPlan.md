# Event Plan

## Nairobi AI Safety Hackathon

**Format:** Two-day in-person hackathon
**Location:** Nairobi, Kenya
**Expected participants:** 40 to 60
**Team size:** 2 to 5 people

## 1. Purpose

The event will introduce more people in Nairobi to practical AI safety work.

The focus is on people who already have some experience with software, machine learning, research, or technology policy.

Rather than making the event primarily lecture based, participants will spend most of their time working on projects.

## 2. Main technical track

The main track will focus on red teaming open source language models.

Participants will test models in controlled environments and investigate specific failure modes.
Possible areas include:

* Prompt injection
* Instruction conflicts
* Hidden instructions in context
* Long context behaviour
* Refusal robustness
* Automated evaluation
* Model comparison

Participants will document their methods and results so that other people can reproduce their work.

## 3. Participant profile

The target audience includes:

* Software engineers
* ML engineers
* Data scientists
* Computer science students
* AI researchers
* Technical founders
* Technology policy researchers

Previous AI safety experience is not required.

Basic programming experience will be useful for the technical challenges, but not every project will require programming.

## 5. Technical setup

Participants will bring their own laptops.

The organizers will provide:

* Reliable Wi-Fi
* Power
* Shared documentation
* Starter code where useful
* Access to selected open source models
* Evaluation scripts where available

Teams will not be expected to run large models locally. Smaller models can be used for many of the proposed challenges.

## 6. Mentors

We plan to invite 4 to 8 mentors with experience in areas such as:

* AI safety
* Machine learning
* LLM evaluation
* Cybersecurity
* AI research
* AI governance

Mentors will help teams define their questions, review methods, troubleshoot problems, and interpret results.

## 7. Judging

Projects will be assessed on:

* Quality of the question
* Execution
* Reproducibility
* Usefulness of the result
* Understanding of limitations

A small, well designed evaluation will be considered more valuable than a complicated project with weak evidence.

## 8. Project outputs

Teams will be encouraged to publish at least one concrete output:

* GitHub repository
* Evaluation dataset
* Experiment results
* Research note
* Prototype
* Policy brief
* Red team report

Projects do not need to be finished products.

## 9. After the event

We will follow up with participants and teams after the hackathon.

For projects that show promise, we may help connect participants with mentors, relevant communities, or further research opportunities.

We will also collect participant feedback and document which projects continue after the event.
